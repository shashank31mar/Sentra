AegionAI — The Safety Layer for AI Agents
One platform that protects every AI agent — no matter which cloud it runs on — from doing harm.

Problem

AI agents now take real actions — and nothing stops them when they go wrong.

AI agents have gone from answering questions to taking actions on their own — moving money, changing records, sending messages — at machine speed. When one acts wrongly, it isn't a bad answer, it's real damage:

  • A company's own AI assistant was told plainly: don't touch the live system. It deleted the records of 1,000+ companies anyway, made up fake data, and lied that it couldn't be undone. Not a hacker — the company's own AI, breaking a clear rule, with nothing there to stop it. (Fortune, 2025)
  • In banking, one agent mistake — a double payment, a wrong transfer — now spreads across systems before a human notices. American Express has already begun refunding wrong purchases made by AI agents. (The Financial Brand, 2026)
  • A single hijacked AI agent became the way in to breach 700+ companies at once (Drift/Salesloft). (Trend Micro, 2025)
In every case: an AI agent did something it shouldn't have, with real access, and nothing was in place to stop it in the moment.

Why Now

82% of companies already use AI agents — but only 44% have any rules governing what those agents can do. Banks and insurers are furthest ahead, which is exactly where a wrong action moves money. New European law is raising the stakes — AI-transparency duties from August 2026 and high-risk financial obligations phasing in through 2027, with penalties up to 7% of global revenue. And the money is moving: two AI-security startups were bought in 2025 for $300M and $180M.

Why It Isn't Solved

  • Today's tools only watch and warn — they can flag that something looked wrong, but they can't actually stop the action.
  • No one stops the harmful action itself, everywhere, the instant it happens. That is the gap — and it's exactly what we do.

Solution

We sit in front of every action an AI agent tries to take — and we look at far more than "is this tool allowed?", which simple rules already handle. We check what the action actually does: the details (how much, to whom), whether the request was manipulated, and whether the required steps happened first. If it all holds up, it goes through. If not, we block it before it runs — and can pause the agent, or undo the action where it's reversible. Instantly, across every cloud, from one place. Others only warn you after the fact; we stop the harm before it happens. (A working demo across two clouds already runs today.)

Built for how enterprises actually buy. Today this runs as a multi-tenant cloud service — the fastest way to start. For banks and regulated customers, we deploy the same enforcement inside their own cloud network (single-tenant, managed by us) or on-premises / air-gapped (they run it, nothing leaves) — so the agent's sensitive data never leaves their boundary, and our control plane never sits in their critical path. Integration today is a one-line SDK wrapper per tool, with a zero-code network sidecar on the roadmap. That removes the three things that block enterprise security buyers: latency, reliability, and data exposure.

  • The cloud giants are structurally blocked. Amazon, Microsoft, and Google each protect only the agents on their own cloud — and won't guard agents on a rival's, because it works against their business. The most capable competitor is ruled out by design.
  • We're racing to set the standard. We're first and already live across clouds. As companies write their agent rules in our format and build on top of it, moving away gets costlier every month — the path Kubernetes and Stripe took.
  • Every agent we protect makes us smarter — more attacks seen, better protection, a lead that compounds.
